Class Modeling with UML

These questions follow the order of Chapter 5. Each of the first eight groups checks one or two sections of the chapter, and the last group reviews the whole chapter. The “Self-check” buttons in the chapter lead to these groups. Nothing you answer is recorded.

What a Class Model Adds and Where It Starts

Read the sections

Self-Check

1. A use case diagram for a plant’s maintenance records draws a figure for each actor. Which label is right for one of those figures?

Yes. Users are named by role, not by person, and a role that uses the system is an actor.

A person may leave or change jobs, and several people may share a role. The diagram names the role.

That is a use case, drawn as an oval inside the system’s boundary, not an actor.

The system is the box that marks the boundary. An actor is outside it and uses it.

2. A plant’s maintenance group is writing the documents for a new database of machine repairs. Which statement about these documents is correct?

That is a requirement. A use case is a function of the system as one of its users sees it, a sequence of steps the user starts to get something done.

That is the work of the use case diagram. The problem statement is a short narrative of why the system is needed, its setting, and what it must do.

Yes. Saying what the system will not do draws its boundary, and a boundary left undrawn grows during design.

It is a requirement about a quality rather than a function, a nonfunctional requirement.

Classes and Attributes

Read the section

Self-Check

1. Which names follow the naming conventions of UML that the book’s class models use? Select every one that does.

Select all that apply.

Yes. A class name is a singular noun with each word capitalized.

Yes. An attribute name begins in lower case, and each later word is capitalized.

A class describes one object, so its name is singular: Customer.

Lower case with underscores is how the book names tables and columns. A class name is written CustomerOrder.

2. Two customers of a class model have the same name, the same city, and the same state, and every other fact recorded about them is the same too. What are they?

Objects are not told apart by their facts alone.

Yes. Two objects remain two even when every recorded fact about them is the same. How the database keeps them apart is decided when the model becomes tables.

Both are customers, with the same kinds of facts, so they belong to one class.

Both are customers. They are two members of the one class Customer.

3. A gauge record keeps its calibration lab’s address as one attribute, “14 Oak St., Tulsa, OK 74103”. The quality department now asks for the gauges calibrated by labs in each state. Which modeling choice does the request call for?

Printing it whole is one use, but the new request needs one part of it on its own. A query would have to pick the state out of the text, and an address typed another way would slip past it.

Yes. The test is whether any query, rule, or sort needs part of the value on its own. The state is now needed alone, so it becomes an attribute with a domain of its own.

Numbered attributes form a repeating group; they do not separate the state from the rest.

Whether a value is atomic depends on its domain and how the business uses it, not on how it looks.

Associations and Multiplicity

Read the section

Self-Check

1. A class model draws WorkCenter 1 -- 0..* Machine. Which sentence reads the 0..* correctly?

This reads the number from the wrong end. The 0..* is written at the Machine end, so it is a count of machines.

Yes. The 0..* is written at the Machine end and counts the machines of one work center. The other sentence, from the 1, is that each machine stands in exactly one work center.

A minimum of 0 means a work center may have no machines.

The 0..* says nothing about how many work centers the plant has.

2. Riverside Hardware placed order SO-5001. In the terms of a class model, what is this fact?

Yes. Links are to an association what objects are to a class: its members.

An order is an object of its own, with facts of its own. The connection between the two is drawn as an association.

A multiplicity is written at an end of an association and counts objects. This fact is one connection, not a count.

A class describes many objects. This fact concerns one customer and one order.

3. An association Truck 0..1 -- 0..* Delivery connects trucks and deliveries. What does the minimum of 0 at the Truck end allow?

That is the minimum at the Delivery end, which counts the deliveries of one truck.

Yes. The minimum at an end decides whether an object at the other end may exist without a link. Here a delivery may have no truck.

The maximum at the Truck end is 1, so a delivery has at most one truck.

The minimum is a rule of the business in the model. Mapped to tables, a minimum of 1 would become a foreign key declared NOT NULL.

Association Classes and Qualified Associations

Read the section

Self-Check

1. At stage D4, StockLevel is drawn as an association class on the association Warehouse 0..* -- 0..* Product, joined to its line by a dashed line. Which statements about it are correct? Select every one that is.

Select all that apply.

Yes. The association class is the association seen as a class, so its objects and the links correspond one to one.

Yes. A stock level is the link between one warehouse and one product, with its quantity on hand and reorder point.

An association class has no multiplicity of its own. The multiplicities are those of the association it is attached to.

Lines of its own would make StockLevel an ordinary class, a different model. The association class already belongs to the association between the two.

2. The cloud instance diagram of the bill of materials draws one cloud for parts in the role of assembly and one for parts in the role of component. The wheel assembly WA-10 appears as a dot in both clouds. What does that mean?

A part number names one part. The two dots are one part drawn twice.

Yes. In a recursive association an object of the class can be linked to another of the same class. A cloud for each role keeps the lines readable, and the role names say which end of each link is which.

Drawing a cloud for each role is a choice made for readability. The role names tell the reader that both clouds hold parts.

Being an assembly and being a component are roles in the bill of materials, not subclasses of Part.

3. A hypothetical ternary association links operators, machines, and work orders, one link for each run, with 0..* at the WorkOrder end. What does that 0..* say?

The multiplicity at an end of a ternary association counts the objects at that end, not at another end.

Yes. At an end of a ternary association, the multiplicity says how many objects at that end may be linked to a given pair of objects at the other two ends.

Multiplicity counts the objects linked to other objects, not the size of a class.

The 0..* is a count of work orders for a given operator and machine; it says nothing about work orders on their own.

4. A class diagram shows a hollow diamond in the middle of the drawing, with a line from it to each of three classes. What does the diamond mark?

Yes. UML draws the association itself as the diamond, with a line to each class it relates.

The diamond of an aggregation sits at the end of a line, against the whole. This diamond stands alone, with three lines leaving it.

Composition is drawn with a filled diamond at the whole’s end of a line.

Generalization is drawn with a hollow triangle at the superclass.

Aggregation and Composition

Read the section

Self-Check

1. In a composition that is not recursive, the multiplicity at the whole’s end is 1. Why?

The number comes from what composition says about each part, not from a drawing rule.

Yes. A minimum of 0 would let a part exist with no whole, and a maximum above 1 would let it belong to two.

The number at the whole’s end counts wholes for one part. A whole may have many parts, as an order has many lines.

A class model shows no keys. The multiplicity states a rule of the business.

2. True or False: UML defines aggregation precisely, so two modelers who follow UML will always draw the hollow diamond in the same places.

UML leaves the exact meaning of aggregation to the modeler. The book fixes its own use: a part that is part of the whole but has a life of its own.

Right. Because UML leaves aggregation’s meaning open, a model that uses it should say what it means by it. This book uses it for a part that can outlive its whole or move to another.

Generalization

Read the section

Self-Check

1. UML names the opposite of {disjoint}. Which keyword allows an object to belong to more than one subclass?

Yes. A part bought in one season and made in another would need {overlapping} in place of {disjoint}.

That keyword is the opposite of {complete}, not of {disjoint}.

{ordered} marks an end of an association whose sequence matters. It has nothing to do with subclasses.

UML gives no such keyword for generalization.

2. In a class diagram, the name of the superclass Part is written in italics. What does that say?

A role is written as a role name at the end of an association, not as a class.

Yes. Every part belongs to some subclass, so Part has no objects apart from theirs. It still holds what the subclasses share.

An eliminated candidate is not drawn at all.

Italics mark an abstract class. A derived attribute is one computed from others, which is a different idea.

3. In a cloud instance diagram, how are the objects of a subclass drawn?

A manufactured part is a part, one object, not two objects joined by a link.

Yes. Each manufactured part is also a part, so its dot lies inside the cloud of Part as well as inside the cloud of ManufacturedPart.

It is the superclass that may have no objects of its own. A subclass’s objects are the ones drawn in its cloud.

The diagram draws it naturally: a cloud inside a cloud.

Building and Reviewing a Class Model

Read the sections

Self-Check

1. An analyst describes a class HandTruckModel as “the HT-100 and the HT-200”. Which test of a class’s soundness does that description fail?

Yes. A class defined only by listing its members is not yet understood. Its description should give the test that decides whether a new model, added next year, belongs.

Uniformity asks whether every object of the class has the same kinds of facts and relationships. A list of two members says nothing either way.

The or test looks for a description that joins unlike things with “or”, which may hide two classes. This description joins two things of one kind.

A list fixes today’s members but gives no rule for tomorrow’s, which is what a class description must supply.

2. A data dictionary holds an entry for each association of a class model as well as for each class and attribute. What does an association’s entry give? Select every one that it does.

Select all that apply.

Yes. The entry begins by saying which association it describes, such as Shipment leaves from Warehouse.

Yes. Each end’s multiplicity is stated in words.

Yes. A reason, such as a shipment being loaded at one dock, lets the business confirm the rule or correct it.

A class model leaves the implementation to the mapping into tables, so the data dictionary of the model names no columns.

3. A reviewer traces the request “which supplier delivered each lot of steel used on a work order” through a class model and finds no path of associations from WorkOrder to Supplier. What does the review conclude?

The joins of a query follow the model’s associations. A request with no path has nothing for the joins to follow.

Yes. A request with no path means the model is missing something. Whether to add it is then a decision for the business.

The list of requests is part of what the business must review; a missing request is a gap in the documents, not a reason to ignore it.

A class model shows relationships as associations, not as reference attributes.

Reading Other Notations

Read the section

Self-Check

1. True or False: the marks of a crow’s foot diagram sit at the opposite ends of a line from where UML writes its multiplicities, so a reader must swap the ends when translating.

The marks sit at the same ends as UML’s numbers, so no swap is needed.

Right. Each end’s marks count the objects at that end, as a UML multiplicity does, so the rule for reading an association as two sentences applies unchanged.

2. A reader finds a data model in another book that draws each relationship as a diamond between the boxes it connects. What is it, and what does this book say about it?

The diamond of an aggregation sits at the end of a line, against the whole, not between two boxes.

Crow’s foot notation draws a relationship as a plain line with marks at its ends.

Yes. Chen’s notation draws relationships as diamonds. A reader who meets it translates it construct by construct, as the book does for crow’s foot notation.

The book’s point is the opposite: other notations say much the same things with different marks.

Review of the Whole Chapter

Read the chapter summary

Self-Check

1. An analyst follows every step of the method for building a class model, from the candidate classes to the data dictionary, with care. The problem statement never mentions the weight of a shipment. What will the model say about weight?

Step 4 finds attributes in the documents and in what the business brings to the model. No step invents a fact that nobody mentioned.

Yes. The method finds only what the documents mention, which is why the documents, like the list of requests, are part of what the business must review.

A derived attribute is computed from facts that are recorded. Nothing recorded gives a weight.

The data dictionary describes the names the model has. It cannot describe a fact that was never named.

2. True or False: the steps of the method for building a class model are done once each, in order, from finding the candidates to writing the data dictionary.

Later steps often send the analyst back to earlier ones.

Right. Eliminating a candidate often turns it into an attribute of another class, and finding an association often shows that its links carry facts, which adds an association class.

3. UML has its own picture of instances, the object diagram, which draws each object as a box. How does the book’s cloud instance diagram compare with it?

Neither picture writes multiplicities. Both draw particular objects and links, from which a multiplicity can be counted.

Yes. Every statement a class diagram makes is a statement about the members of sets, and the clouds draw the sets.

A cloud instance diagram shows a few objects at a moment. The class diagram states the rules every set of objects must follow.

A cloud instance diagram labels each dot with a business identifier, such as a customer number; it shows no keys chosen for tables.
